Aldaron calls it transformation equity. First deal: majority stake in a firm with 2,000+ remnant-asset transactions

A new control firm says the team that underwrites the deal is the same team that builds the AI. The first test case is Oak Point Partners.

LW

LatentWire Staff

Oct 20, 2026 · 2:00 PM ET · 4 min read

Private equity has spent years talking about AI inside the hold period. Aldaron is trying to productize that talk into a firm structure: control capital plus a Deployment Team of engineers who stay after closing.

On October 15, the San Francisco and New York firm announced its launch and a majority equity investment in Oak Point Partners, which it calls the leading institutional purchaser of remnant assets. The deal closed in March 2026. Terms were not disclosed. PE Hub and other trade coverage treated the announcement as Aldaron's first disclosed transaction.

Aldaron brands the strategy "transformation equity." In the release, that means world-class AI engineering paired with control-oriented investing. A hybrid Deployment Team of engineers and investment professionals works alongside management at each partner company to rework how the business operates and to build systems that create equity value through applied AI.

What the model claims to fix

The pitch is alignment. Transformation done well, Aldaron says, needs deep technology expertise, operational fluency, full-time dedication, and a structure that lines up with management. The firm says the team that underwrites an investment is the same team that builds the technology, drives the change, and is measured on whether it succeeds.

That is a direct answer to a familiar PE complaint: operating partners and vendor pilots that never become how the portco runs. Aldaron's language is careful on labor. It says durable companies will put technology in people's hands and transform how work gets done — amplifying, not replacing, experience and know-how built over decades.

Who is underwriting and building

The co-founders are Will Oberndorf, Evan Mendez and Jay Grewal. Collectively, the firm says the Aldaron team has more than 25 years of experience at Palantir, plus private markets, strategy and change management. Grewal co-founded Palantir's commercial business, pioneered forward-deployed engineering in commercial markets, scaled and led a team of more than 600 forward-deployed engineers, and co-created Foundry. Mendez was on Carlyle's technology buyout team and an early member of that firm's AI team. Oberndorf spent the past decade building the private markets practice at Oberndorf Enterprises, investing in early- and growth-stage technology businesses that serve traditional industries.

In a joint quote, the founders said they set out to build a firm that does more than provide capital — one that partners deeply so portfolio companies can "go on offense" with frontier technology. The economic model, they say, creates strong alignment with the investments.

Calling it transformation equity does not settle whether the hold period changes — only whether someone stayed after the close.

Why Oak Point is the first case

Oak Point, based outside Chicago and led by founder and CEO Eric Linn, pioneered the remnant-asset category in 2006. The company has completed more than 2,000 transactions, buying intangible assets and payment rights from Fortune 500 companies, private equity-backed businesses and distressed entities. Linn said existing investors remain invested.

Aldaron's Deployment Team is layering AI and machine learning on that foundation — accelerating discovery of remnant assets across Oak Point's 1,000-plus asset channels and increasing operational capacity. Mendez called Oak Point the kind of business Aldaron was built for: proprietary data, deep domain expertise, and process knowledge that cannot be replicated but can be amplified with technology applied the right way.

The lesson for everyone else

Linn's quote is the clearest picture of the operating model. He said Aldaron's team is embedded, operating alongside Oak Point's people, and learning workflows from the inside out — not only bringing capital and technology. That is the bar any "AI value creation" slide has to clear: someone learns how the work actually moves before they automate a step.

For other sponsors, the announcement is less a proof point than a structure to watch. Most hold-period AI programs still start with a tool and a pilot. Aldaron's claim is that underwriting, building and accountability sit in one team measured on whether the change sticks. Whether that produces a multiple, or only a cleaner narrative at exit, is not in the release.

What we still don't know

The purchase price or equity percentage behind "majority." Fund size and LP base — the Business Wire release does not disclose them. How large the Deployment Team is on Oak Point today, or how success is measured in the first year. Whether remnant-asset discovery gains show up as higher recovery rates, faster cycle time, or both. Whether the model generalizes beyond a data-heavy niche platform to a classic industrial or services portco.

Aldaron's own frame is that the underwriters stay to build. The first disclosed partner is a company with two decades of process and more than 2,000 closed transactions. That is a strong place to start. It is not yet a dataset on hold-period returns.
